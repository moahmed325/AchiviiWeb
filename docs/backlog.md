States: `IN PROGRESS` · `PAUSED` · `WAITING` (on something outside the code) · `STUB` (problem written down, not planned) · `PLANNED`.

## How it works

**Who adds items.** Mo's main chat, when it reviews a milestone report and sorts its "Problems found", or whenever Mo or anyone finds a problem. Implementing chats do not edit this file; they list problems in their report.

**Sizes.**
- **small**: the fix is known, fits in one branch, and needs no product decision (a one-line choice Mo can make in the prompt is fine). Fixed on the quick-fix track.
- **big**: needs a product decision, more than two milestones, a change to stored data, or it is unclear what the fix is. Becomes a feature (start at `01-brief.md`, or at `02-feature.md` when the direction is obvious).
- **owner**: something only Mo can do by hand (a production setting, a database command, a manual check).

**Statuses.** `OPEN` · `FIXING (branch)` · `DONE (commit)` · `MOVED → docs/features/<name>` · `DROPPED (why)`. Append new items at the bottom; never renumber. More than one chat may add items on the same day: pull the latest `main` and take the next number after the last row just before you write, and check for a duplicate ID before you push.

**Urgent problems** (users are hitting it now) do not wait: fix them right away on the quick-fix track, and add a line here marked DONE.

### Quick-fix track

1. Pick one small item, or a few that touch the same area.
2. Give a chat a short prompt:

```text
Quick fix for backlog item B-<n> (docs/backlog.md): <what is wrong, where, and what "fixed" looks like>.
<Exact copy in quotes, if any. Mo's choice, if one was needed.>
Branch: fix-b<n>-<short-name>, from main. Push to that branch, never to main.
Checks: <tests to add or run; baseline numbers>.
Other chats are working on: <files or areas not to touch>.
```

3. The chat follows "Quick fixes" in `CLAUDE.md` and reports in its reply.
4. Review it, merge, and set the item to `DONE (commit)`.
