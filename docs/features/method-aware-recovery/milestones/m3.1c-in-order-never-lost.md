### Goal
With the switch on, every step of an in-order move kind is either done, held by a later session this week in the right order, or listed as not done for next week. After a break, the first hard session back is marked easy. With the switch off, everything is exactly as today.

### Requirements
1. **The queue (RULE-20, MR-30 (1)).** For each in-order kind whose action is move, a pure function works out the steps of that kind not done yet, in order: first those on days of this week that closed undone (missed days, days of a break, a lower-priority step, a step that was carried or shifted there and missed again), then the ones on the remaining sessions as written. Export it, because M3.1d will use the same function at the week's end to tell next week (do not wire it into `writeNextWeek` here). It is worked out from the stored week (steps, markers, day status), so a replan with nothing new gives the same result.
2. **Reorder inside the week.** The week's remaining sessions of that kind (today or later, not done, not closed, not the test day) keep their days and take the queue's steps in order. Each placed step must fit its day: the day never gets longer, a fixed, continue-marked, swapped or in-order step of another kind is never trimmed (MR-29 (4)), and the rest gap holds as MR-29 (2) and (5) say. If a session can't take the next step safely, it gives up its own step of the kind (that step joins the queue in its place, order kept) and the next session is tried (MR-30 (5): safety first, then order, then keep going). The steps left over are not done this week (requirement 5). Each step that changed session stores a marker inside `detailedSteps` (keep `shiftedFrom` or rename it; say which). Every changed day of one reorder is written in one transaction, all or nothing, as M3.1b's shift is.
3. **This replaces the one-step shift.** `planShift`, `shift_blocked` and `carriedFrom.pushedOut` go, or are reshaped; say what replaced each. A missed day whose only move step is in order follows the reorder; its other steps keep their own actions (RULE-9 to RULE-12 for kinds that are not in order). MR-26 and MR-28 (2) still apply to steps that are not of an in-order move kind.
4. **After a break (MR-30 (1), ND-11).** The in-order steps on open-gap days join the queue like any other. Nothing else moves out of the break, and gap days keep `in_gap`.
5. **Not done this week (MR-30 (6)).** The steps left over at the end of the queue are counted in `weekCounts.toNextWeek` (replacing `pushedOut`; present only when the plan has `outcomes`). A missed day whose in-order step got no session this week gets a new outcome value (for example `next_week`) so M3.3 can show "next week starts with it". The body lists them in order per kind.
6. **Easy first hard session back (RULE-21, MR-31 (1)).** When there is an open gap, code puts exactly "First hard session after a few days off: keep the effort easy today." first in the instructions of the first hard step on the first open practice day this week after the gap that has a hard step. Once only (not again on a replan), the minutes unchanged, a guarded write, only when the switch is on and the carry switch allows writes.
7. **Warm-up line only on physical steps (MR-31 (2)).** MR-26's line goes on a moved or reordered step only when it is hard or the goal's profile template is `endurance` or `strength`. Never on lessons, writing, coding or other steps.
8. **Tests.** Generated weeks (extend `methodOrderGap.test.ts`) with missed days, breaks, lower-priority in-order steps, steps missed twice and replans check after every plan: no in-order step lost (each is done, in a later session in order, or listed as not done, in order); order kept; no day longer; nothing protected trimmed; the rest gap; nothing on the test day or a rest day; a replan changes nothing. Plus named tests: lessons 4 to 6 missed in a break, the rest of the week does 4, 5, 6 and 7 to 9 are listed for next week; a lower-priority lesson on a missed day is not lost; a moved lesson missed again is not lost; the last lesson of the week missed is listed for next week; a session too short for the next lesson gives up its own and the next one is tried; the easy line once, on the right step; the warm-up line on a strength step and not on a lesson; a reorder whose transaction loses one guard writes nothing; switch off, no profile, or untagged steps give exactly today's plan and body.
