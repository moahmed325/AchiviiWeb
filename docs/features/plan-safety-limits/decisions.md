# Plan Safety Limits — Decisions

**Prefix:** PSL

How this log works: `docs/templates/decisions.md`.

### PSL-1 — A stopgap in the week call ships first, and a second breach fails the week (2026-10-17)
**Question:** what protects live run10k and recomp users while the feature is built (`01-brief.md` section 10), and what happens when the model's second answer still breaks a limit?
**Decision:** (1) A quick fix (B-63) in `backend/src/lib/ai/weekPlan.ts` and `backend/src/lib/ai/roadmap.ts` (changed 2026-10-17): a safety-limits block in the week prompt for high-load goals (run10k, recomp), which in weeks 1 to 3 says plainly that it overrides the method's reps-in-reserve range; a narrow scan of the text of every high-load step, meaning a step of a high-load goal or any step flagged `highLoad`, custom goals included; and the same max-test and to-failure scan on weeks 1 to 3's tests in the roadmap. A hit is a hard reason: it gives one retry, and if the second answer still breaks a limit the week fails (the honest 503 at goal create; "This week is unchanged" 503 at review). (2) The scan must not count safe wording ("stop 3 reps before failure", "never train to failure", "no max test this week", "keep it under 80% of 1RM"), with tests that show it. (3) Every hit is logged as a `[Safety]` line with the goal, the week, the rule and the matched text, so the false-alarm rate can be checked in production.
**Why (Mo):** an unsafe week must never reach the person, and a false alarm that blocks a safe week must be visible and rare.
**Changes:** `01-brief.md` section 10; `docs/backlog.md` B-63. Changed 2026-10-17 (Mo): roadmap tests, custom goals' flagged steps and the recomp override line added.

### PSL-2 — Weeks 1 to 3: at least 3 reps in reserve (2026-10-17)
**Question:** the old limit says at least 3 reps in reserve in weeks 1 to 3; the recomp method allows 1 to 3. Which holds?
**Decision:** at least 3 reps in reserve in weeks 1 to 3, for the stopgap and as the starting number for the feature (`01-brief.md` section 9, question 6). From week 4 the method's own range applies (recomp: 1 to 3).
**Why (Mo):** 3 is inside recomp's own 1 to 3 range, the easy end while the body adapts, so it tightens the start without contradicting the method.
**Changes:** `01-brief.md` sections 9 and 10.

### PSL-3 — The stored weekly test is logged, not refused (2026-10-17)
**Question:** B-63 review: the test day runs the roadmap's test as written, and a goal stored before B-63 may hold a week 2 or 3 test that breaks a limit ("Test your 1RM squat"). Refusing it would fail that week's call on every review. What happens?
**Decision:** in the week call, a breach that the week's stored test itself holds is logged as `[Safety]` with outcome `test_text` and not refused (on the test step for any rule the test breaks; elsewhere only for a sentence copied from it). New roadmaps cannot store such a test: the roadmap refuses a week test that breaks any rule the week call checks, in every week. Fixing stored tests on older goals belongs to the feature.
**Why:** a refusal there leaves the person stuck at the review for good; the log shows which goals hold such a test.
**Changes:** `backend/src/lib/ai/weekPlan.ts` (`safetyBreaches`), `roadmap.ts`; CLAUDE.md step 6.

### PSL-4 — The limit numbers, step load and no switch (2026-10-17)
**Question:** `02-feature.md` section 4 proposed every limit number; section 7 adds load fields to stored steps; should the limits have an on/off switch?
**Decision:** (1) All the numbers in `02-feature.md` section 4's table, as proposed: running distance target +10% a week or +1 km; running time target at most 2% faster a week; lift load target +5% a week or +2.5 kg; body weight loss at most 1% a week, gain at most 0.5%; body fat at most 0.5 points a week; deficit 250 to 600 kcal a day; daily intake never below 1,200 kcal; running total +10% on the base week or +10 minutes / +1 km; longest run at most 50% of the week; at most 2 hard runs a week; weeks 1 to 3 lifting as PSL-2; base week = last week's planned total, or what was done when under 70% of it. (2) High-load steps store an optional `load` (additive keys in the stored step JSON, no migration). (3) No switch: the limits are always on.
**Why (Mo):** agreed as proposed; a safety rule should not be possible to turn off.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` sections 4, 5 and 7 (status APPROVED).

### PSL-8 — Load is required only on run and lift steps (2026-10-19)
**Question:** M1.1 Q11: `isHighLoadStep` counts every step of a run10k or recomp goal (warm-ups, food logging), and the stored `highLoad` flag depends on goal age. Which steps must carry `load` (RULE-4)?
**Decision:** steps the model flags `highLoad` and steps of a run or lift kind. Not every step of a pathway goal: a "log your meals" or warm-up step needs no load. The test step on a running goal gives its load whatever its flag (Q9, PSL-11).
**Why (Mo):** otherwise ordinary steps cause retries.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` RULE-4; `03-phases.md` M2.1.

### PSL-10 — The target families (2026-10-19)
**Question:** M1.1 Q4, Q5, Q16: pace, continuous running minutes, waist, cumulative weight lost, % of 1RM and RIR targets fit no family as written.
**Decision:** (1) Pace (min/km, lower) is the running time family. (2) Continuous running minutes (higher) uses the running distance rule in minutes (+10% or +10 minutes). (3) A time or minutes target counts as running only on run10k or when the metric names a run; otherwise `unknown_family`. (4) Waist and cumulative weight lost: no family, logged `unknown_family`. (5) Body fat in `%` or `percent` is the body fat family (percentage points). (6) % of 1RM and RIR targets: no family; RULE-5 covers them on steps.
**Why (Mo):** agreed as recommended.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` sections 3 and 5.

### PSL-12 — Older goals on read: the honest line, the log and the rebuilt text (2026-10-20)
**Question:** writing M1.3's prompt: when does the week 12 line show and what fills it; how does RULE-10's "each lowered target" apply when an older goal is lowered on every read; what of `keyMilestone` and a pathway's tests is rebuilt?
**Decision:** (1) The line shows whenever week 12 was lowered, on a new or an older goal. When the goal text names a number the safe week 12 no longer meets, it is the full line with that number as {goal}; otherwise only "Week 12 aims at {safe}, a safe step." (the lowered number was the model's, not theirs). {safe} and {goal} are the rounded amount and unit, with no metric and no "or less". (2) For older goals, RULE-10 is read as: one `[Safety]` `target_step` line, outcome `lowered_on_read`, when a week whose target was lowered on read is written (`writeNextWeek`); nothing is logged when a goal is only shown (`presentGoal`). (3) Only weeks whose target changed are rebuilt. On a pathway with tests, the measured week's test is refilled from the preset, and `finalTest` too when week 12 changed. `keyMilestone` is rebuilt from the refilled test; on a custom goal (and an older pathway goal whose stored test is not the preset's own) only the pass mark changes, in `keyMilestone` and in `test.passIf` as PSL-7 says, and the test's instructions and `finalTest` stay (M1.3 review). For "lose N", {goal} is the weight it implies (starting point minus N), or the short line with no starting point (M1.3 review). (4) Everything that compares a result with a target uses the lowered target (on 2026-10-20 only the app compares, and it reads targets from `presentGoal`).
**Why (Mo):** the full line would credit the person with a number they never asked for; logging on every read would flood the log; text code did not write stays as stored.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` section 6; `milestones/m1.3-older-goals-honest-line.md` Prompt.

### PSL-13 — Older goals: closed weeks stay as stored, and the climb is checked from the last one (2026-10-20)
**Question:** M1.3 report, Stop-if (2) and the review's round 2: lowering every week on read puts a closed week's lowered target beside the result logged against the stored one, and checking from the starting point can show an open week below a week already done.
**Decision:** a closed week (`completed`, or jumped) keeps its stored target and text and is never lowered. The weeks after the last closed week are checked from its stored target, one safe step a week (on a light-test goal, from the last closed of weeks 4 and 8, four safe steps to the next big week; a closed light week is never the starting value); with none closed, from the starting point. `presentGoal` and `writeNextWeek` use the same rule. A week 12 lowered only on read gets the line only when this rule lowers it; one lowered at create keeps it.
**Why (Mo):** a result keeps the target it was judged against, and the rest of the plan climbs safely from where the person is, not from a past they already lived through. M2.2's base week (what was done) guards a closed week that was itself too steep.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` RULE-9; `milestones/m1.3-older-goals-honest-line.md` Review.

### PSL-14 — Load on cardio steps, the hard-run mark, and km as written (2026-10-20)
**Question:** M2.1 review: a custom weight-loss goal gets the strength template, whose cardio kind is `conditioning`, not a run kind, so its runs were asked for a load only if the model flagged them `highLoad`. And code shortens a step's minutes after the model; should `runKm` shrink too?
**Decision:** (1) On a load goal, every run step and every lift step needs a load whatever the target's family, and a `conditioning` step needs some load (option A). (2) Any load that names `runMinutes` or `runKm` also gives `hardRun`, whatever the step's need. (3) `runMinutes` scales with the step's minutes (PSL-11 Q10); `runKm` stays as written, and M2.2 must not scale it down.
**Why (Mo):** otherwise a weight-loss goal's runs and strength sessions are left out and M2.2 cannot check them; keeping km as written while the minutes shrink over-counts the planned distance, which is the safe side.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` RULE-4; `03-phases.md` M2.2; `backend/src/lib/safety/stepLoad.ts`.

### PSL-15 — How M2.2 counts a week's running, and M2.2 in two parts (2026-10-21)
**Question:** M2.1 report, "Questions for M2.2" 1 to 6: what a step with no load counts as, a `runMinutes` above the step's length, km and minutes in one week, a run on a rest day, profile-call kinds, and a `conditioning` step with no running. And M2.2 is large.
**Decision:** (1) **Steps with no load.** In the week being checked, a run step (a run kind, or the model's test step on a running goal) kept without a load on the last attempt counts its whole `durationMinutes` as running, and a `quality_session` counts as a hard run; a lift step with no numbers is left to RULE-8's text scan. A code-made test step on a running goal counts its minutes as running, and in a measured (not light) week as one hard run; the light step counts as easy running. A code-made 10-minute version done instead of a run (`usedMinimumVersion`) counts its minutes, at most 10, as running. **A base week is never 0 because its running is unknown:** when any run step of the base week, planned or done, has no load (a step kept on the last attempt, or any week written before M2.1), the week has no base: the running-total check is skipped as in week 1 (RULE-5), logged once as `[Safety]` `no_base`, and every other limit still applies. A base week whose runs all carry a load and that ran 0 is a real 0 (the allowance only, `02-feature.md` section 5). (2) A `runMinutes` above the step's `durationMinutes` is counted as written and logged. (3) A running total is in km only when every run in it gives `runKm`; otherwise in minutes, using `runMinutes` where given and `durationMinutes` where not. When the base week and this week come out in different units, both are compared in minutes, by the same rule. (4) A run on a rest day counts in the running total. (5) Profile-call kinds marked `highLoad` needing a load stay in B-89, outside M2.2, and B-89 must be done before `CUSTOM_RECOVERY_PROFILES_ENABLED` is ever turned on. (6) A `conditioning` step counts as a run only when its load gives `runMinutes` or `runKm` above 0. (7) **Longest run.** The 50% rule applies only to a week with 3 or more runs; in a 1 or 2 run week the running-total limit caps it. A week with no base (week 1, or an unknown base under (1)) and fewer than 3 runs has no running-total check, so a fixed cap holds its longest run: 30 minutes, or 5 km when the week is counted in km (from run10k's week 1, "first 30-minute continuous run", and its beginner level, "3 to 5 km continuously"; `02-feature.md` section 5 names no number). Both numbers live in `lib/safety/limits.ts`. (8) M2.2 is split: M2.2a, the week checks against the base week (RULE-5, RULE-6, B-64, the running line, AC-5, AC-6, AC-10 weeks); M2.2b, the first week back and the older weeks it reads (RULE-7, PSL-11 Q13, AC-7).
**Why (Mo):** an unknown count is counted strictly in the week's own check, but never as a zero base: 0 × 1.1 = 0 would refuse every next week and give 503s. The 50% rule would refuse almost any 1 or 2 run week, and without a base such a week still needs a cap on a single run. Two units can only be compared in one, and every run step has minutes.
**Changes:** `03-phases.md` M2.2a, M2.2b; `docs/backlog.md` B-89; `milestones/m2.2a-week-limits.md` Prompt. PSL-11 Q10's scaling is for shortening only since PSL-17 (3). Amended by PSL-16 (2026-10-21): a known 0-run base is held like week 1, not to the allowance.

### PSL-16 — A base week of 0 runs is held like week 1 (2026-10-21)
**Question:** M2.2a review: a known base week that ran 0 (missed, not ticked, or a break before M2.2b) allows only the 10-minute allowance next week. A run10k week can rarely be written inside it, so every try of the close gives the same 503 and the person can stay stuck on "Couldn't get your next week ready". And how is the model's own measured test step counted when it comes without a load?
**Decision:** (1) A known base of 0 runs is held like week 1: no running-total check; the 50% rule with 3 or more runs; and every run at most the fixed cap (30 minutes, or 5 km when the week is counted in km, PSL-15 (7)), whatever the number of runs. When both apply, the lower limit holds. (2) The model's own test step on a running goal, kept without a load, counts as a hard run in a measured week (the stored test is not light and the week is not a high-load first week back), as code's measured test does; in a light week it is easy. (3) A week with no load on any step is read as written before M2.1, so its base is unknown, even when it really ran 0 (accepted: there is no stored way to tell the two apart, and with (1) both are held almost the same). `no_base` is logged only on a running goal, or when a run step of the base week had no load. (4) The handoff never places a step whose load names running; it waits as a step that doesn't fit does (the week's running was checked before placement).
**Why (Mo):** a restart at week 1's level is safe and can always be written; a limit no week can meet only gives 503s. A measured test is a hard effort whoever wrote it.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` section 4 (the numbers: longest run) and section 5 (0 runs); `milestones/m2.2a-week-limits.md` Review; `backend/src/lib/safety/weekLoad.ts`, `recovery/handoff.ts`.

### PSL-17 — The week limits are told on every attempt, and a safe first answer is kept (2026-10-21)
**Question:** a live Gemini check before pushing M2.2a: run10k week 5 (base 150, limit 165) failed 3 of 3 with a 503. Attempt 1 is almost always refused first for a day-level reason ("needs 2 to 4 steps"), and `checkWeekAnswer` returned on it before the load checks, so a running-total breach was first seen on the last attempt and ended the call. After that was fixed, week 5's attempt 1 was within the limit and refused only for a one-step day, and attempt 2 went over.
**Decision:** (1) On a load goal, an attempt refused for a day-level reason is still checked against the safety limits on every day that parses, and those reasons go first in the retry reason; each breach is logged once, `retry` (`failed` on the last attempt). (2) When the last attempt is refused and every hard reason is a safety breach, attempt 1's answer is checked again as a last attempt (a one-step day kept, a missing test step added); if it passes every check, safety included, it is the week, logged once as `[Safety]` outcome `first_answer`. Nothing unsafe is ever returned and there is no extra model call; RULE-6's one retry stands. (3) When code makes a step longer (the last-attempt stretch of a lone step, `fitStepMinutes` growing a step), `load.runMinutes` does not grow; it still scales down when a step is shortened (amends PSL-11 Q10: code never adds running).
**Why (Mo):** a limit the model never hears about can't be met, and a safe answer should not be thrown away for a layout point while an unsafe one ends the call. Live check after the fix: 27 of 27 week calls written across run10k weeks 2, 4 to 8, recomp weeks 2 and 5 and a custom weight-loss week 2 (week 5 through `first_answer` 3 of 3).
**Changes:** `backend/src/lib/ai/weekPlan.ts` (`dayExit`, `dayLevelReason`, `firstAnswer`), `lib/safety/stepLoad.ts` (`withMinutes`); `milestones/m2.2a-week-limits.md` Review; CLAUDE.md steps 5 and 6.

### PSL-18 — The first week back: no allowance, and a floor of 30 minutes / 5 km (2026-10-21)
**Question:** writing M2.2b's prompt: RULE-7 holds the first week back to the last done running total. Does it get the +10% allowance, and what happens when that total is tiny (one 10-minute run), which no run10k week can fit, so the close would 503 on every try, while someone who never ran gets the looser week-1 hold?
**Decision:** (1) No allowance: the first week back runs at most what was last done, as RULE-7 says and as the FIRST WEEK BACK block already tells the writer ("at or below what they last completed"). (2) A floor: the most allowed is never below 30 minutes, or 5 km when the week is compared in km; a named constant in `lib/safety/limits.ts` (the same numbers as PSL-15 (7)'s cap). (3) The week read is the newest week up to the closing week with any done running, counted by done only (no 70% rule); weeks with no done running are passed over; a done run with no running number makes the base unknown (PSL-15 (1)), and no week with done running is a known 0 (PSL-16). (4) It applies to every load goal whose new week is a first week back (`firstWeekBack` given), not only high-load ones. (5) After a break, an unknown base is not "no total check": the week is held as PSL-16 holds a 0-run base (every run at most 30 minutes / 5 km, the 50% rule with 3 or more runs), and `no_base` is logged as now. (6) Stopgap until weekly-update M3.4 sets the restart level: after a break of 21 days or more (`LONG_BREAK_DAYS`, WU-35 (3)), the first week back may run at most half of the base week's done total (a named share in `limits.ts`), never below the floor; `first_week_back_base` logs the share used. M3.4 replaces this share. (7) Reading the older weeks is not caught: a failed read fails the close with a 5xx (the route's own catch answers 500) and saves nothing, and the restart level stays M3.4's. (8) M2.2b review: where a running-total limit holds on a running goal, the code-made light test step swapped in on the last attempt takes at most the whole running minutes left under the limit, never longer than it was, and is not shortened below a 10-minute version (`MINIMUM_VERSION_MINUTES`); with less room the week fails as before. An unknown base after a break is told and refused as "last week's running isn't known".
**Why (Mo):** a limit no week can meet only gives 503s, and having run a little must never hold a person tighter than not having run at all. After a break, not knowing what was run must not lift the limits, and three weeks or more away calls for less than what was last done.
**Changes:** `02-feature.md` section 4 (the numbers: base week); `03-phases.md` M2.2b; weekly-update M3.4 (replaces (6)); `backend/src/lib/safety/weekLoad.ts`, `lib/ai/weekPlan.ts` (`withLightSteps`, `runningLimitMinutes`); `milestones/m2.2b-first-week-back.md` Prompt.
