### Decisions I made
1. **A "done step" for the length** is a practice day that is `completed`, not a rest day and not a day not offered. Other option: any completed row. Why: a rest day logged complete is not practice, and a day not offered was never shown.
2. **High-load for the line** is `isHighLoadGoal`, a `highLoad` flag, or a `highLoad` kind; hard is `actionOf`. Other option: the step's flag only. Why: matches how the safety check reads high-load (`safetyBreaches`, ND-5), and a step of a high-load kind is high-load even when the model forgot the flag.
3. **No readable profile on a high-load goal**: every high-load step gets the line. Other option: none. Why: nothing says which steps are hard, and the safer side is the easy line.
4. **The test step** gets the line only when it is hard by `actionOf` (flagged high-load), as RULE-21 does (MR-32 (5)). Other option: never on the test step. Why: the spec says every hard high-load step, and run10k's light test is the long run.
5. **"Away N days" is `breakDays`** (from the last done step), not the gap. Other option: the gap. Why: it is how long they were really away (WU-18).
6. **The block goes last in the prompt**, after WHERE THEY ARE, in both prompt paths. Other option: before the kinds block. Why: the prompt without a break is then exactly a prefix, easy to check.
7. **The rule entry is logged, not stored.** Other option: store it. Why: no field for it; M3.4 can work it out from `breakDays` and the profile.
8. **Both paths**: the review after a break also stores `breakDays`, tells the writer and adds the line (it keeps its own insight). Other option: automatic close only. Why: `writeWeekAfter` and `saveWeekClose` are shared, as M1.4's move is.
9. **Welcome back for every break**, even when the limit was used up and nothing moved. Other option: the neutral line then. Why: the prompt's Out of scope says a break past the limit saves the welcome-back line.
10. **A RULE-21 target holding the new line gets nothing** (no easy start record). Other option: give RULE-21's line to the next hard step without it. Why: the first hard step back already says keep it easy; a second line later in the week would be RULE-21's wrong wording (WU-35).
11. **The app shows the new line as a lead line** (`stepLines.ts`), Design.md updated. Other option: leave it in the paragraph. Why: it is stored the same way as RULE-21's line, which the app already lifts out.
